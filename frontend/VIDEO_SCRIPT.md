# Marswalk short demo script

Aim for a 28–29 second export. Read this aloud and time the actual delivery;
the shot timings below are editing targets, not a measured voice recording.

## Spoken script

Could you walk here on Mars?

Looks easy from above. Up close? Different story.

Marswalk compares NASA terrain maps with Perseverance rover observations to
reveal what broad maps can miss.

Choose stops, plan around obstacles, and check walking limits.

Our goal: help future explorers understand where they could walk—and where
the evidence isn’t strong enough.

Marswalk.

## Shots and captions

| Target time | Shot | Caption / editing cue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–3 s | Rotate the 3D terrain, then reveal the included walk. | “Could you walk here?” |
| 3–7 s | Switch from a 64 m slope comparison to 4 m. Use a rehearsed cell where the shorter reading is steeper. | Brief meme: “looks walkable” → “the terrain: 👀”. Show actual readings; keep the supplied verdict unchanged. |
| 7–14 s | Show the Sol 700 reference, map slope, measured rover tilt and photo. | “Perseverance brought receipts.” Smaller credit: “HiRISE terrain · NASA/USGS / Perseverance · NASA/JPL-Caltech”. |
| 14–22 s | Add two science stops, trigger obstacle-aware planning, cut to the real returned route and its readings. | “Proposed astronaut walk”. Cut out waiting time, without fabricating results. |
| 22–26 s | Begin the goal sentence over the actual verdict, readable reason and an uncertainty or unavailable-data statement. | Let the evidence stay readable; no meme here. |
| 26–29 s | Clean route overview and Marswalk logo. | “Planning prototype · Not mission safety certification”. Add a short readable project URL if available. |

Use the two meme captions as quick visual beats, not extra spoken lines.
Skip camera-search results, settings, metadata and export controls in this cut.
The 3D terrain is reconstructed from coarse samples; do not imply a detailed
astronaut-eye simulation. The rover comparison does not establish astronaut safety.
